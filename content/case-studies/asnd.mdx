<CsSection num="01" label="Context" title={<>hiring a creative still starts with <em>"who do you know?"</em></>}>

a referral, a dm, a favor. it works, right up until you don't already know the right person.

asnd (say it "ascend") is an invite-only, vetted network where brands and agencies find creative collaborators by craft, not follower count. it is India-first, starting in Mumbai, and it is deliberately not a marketplace, an agency, or a social feed. it is the curated discovery layer that sits under how good creative work actually gets made: vetted by people, not algorithms.

the market is there. India has 100M+ creators, growing 20%+ a year, inside a creator economy already worth about $2.5B.

<Caption>source: KPMG · Redseer; Kofluence / IBEF, 2025.</Caption>

but for anyone trying to hire, discovery is broken in four familiar ways:

<ProblemGrid>
<ProblemCard title="slow">post a brief, dm dozens, chase replies, and maybe find a fit three to four weeks later.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="noise">search "video editor, Mumbai" and get 12,840 results with no filters, no ranking, no vetting, all looking the same.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="no trust">followers, reach, and engagement don't tell you the things that matter: will they reply, hit the deadline, or be good to work with.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="gatekept">the paid work sits behind invite-only rooms. talented people who are skilled, ready, and unseen have no referral, so no way in.</ProblemCard>
</ProblemGrid>

<InsightBand kicker="the insight">the pattern underneath all four: discovery is a <em>trust</em> problem, not a search problem. the shape is trust to work to more work, never platform to discovery to work. curation creates value, not volume.</InsightBand>

</CsSection>

<CsSection num="02" label="Research & Synthesis" title={<>what the <em>interviews</em> revealed.</>}>

<Chips items={["90+ interviews, and counting", "creators · brands · agencies", "6 personas", "opportunity map"]} />

our team has run **more than 90 founder-led interviews** across creators, brands, and agencies, and we're still going, driven by my co-founder Aryan. my job was synthesis: i clustered them into six personas, an opportunity map, and the information architecture the whole product is built on. three findings did the most to shape the design.

<Quote cite="Priyanshu · content creator + director">i interviewed around 20 to 25 people just to find the one. it was a very difficult task.</Quote>

<Quote cite="Shruti · brand-side">i don't need the biggest name. i need someone i know will deliver.</Quote>

<Quote cite="Komal · musician">the talent's out there, you just can't find it. a 2 to 5% cut? gladly, if you do the finding for me.</Quote>

those three lines are the product in miniature: discovery is the bottleneck, trust beats reach, and people will pay to skip the search. the synthesis also found the sharpest wedge. money flows brands to agencies to creators, and the agencies in the middle feel the pain daily while holding the budget. so we built for them first.

<CsSection num="03" label="Positioning & Principles" title={<>craft over <em>follower count</em>.</>}>

the positioning fell out of the research, not a whiteboard. for brands, agencies, and creators who need the right fit, asnd is a curated, trust-based discovery network that surfaces relevant, vetted people ready to start work, unlike Instagram, referrals, cold dms, and open marketplaces. it is a discovery layer, not a marketplace, an agency, or a feed. you don't need more people. you need the right one, and a reason to believe them.

to hold that promise honest in the interface, i wrote six operating principles the whole product obeys:

<ProblemGrid>
<ProblemCard title="squint test">the point of a section lands in one glance, even blurred.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="no suspense">value reads immediately, never slow-revealed behind a scroll.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="show, don't tell">don't assert a problem, show it with the real quote, number, or screen.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="signal over vanity">craft, fit, reliability, vouches, past brands. never follower counts. this is the whole product in one line.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="motion serves the story">static by default, animate only to advance a point, and honor reduced-motion always.</ProblemCard>
<ProblemCard title="low cognitive load">the fewest words that deliver the message. cut the second sentence if the first one lands.</ProblemCard>
</ProblemGrid>

</CsSection>

<CsSection num="04" label="The Design" title={<>from a 2026 cliché to a <em>committed</em> system.</>}>

the first pass looked fine and said nothing: warm beige, a gold accent, playfair, a little lavender. calm, safe, and the most saturated brand cliché of the year. the pivot came from a blunt co-founder note, "the storytelling keeps getting lost in the execution," so i rebuilt the identity around one rule, design serves the story, and picked a system decisive enough to enforce it.

<Directions>
<Direction n="01" title="the calm-and-gold start">warm beige, gold, playfair, pastels. later flagged as the rejected ai-slop look. pretty, and forgettable.</Direction>
<Direction n="02" title="an electric alternate">a bricolage grotesque and electric-coral branding spec, explored and documented as a target identity, ink-led and decisive.</Direction>
<Direction n="03" title="the committed system" chosen>blue + cormorant garamond. one ink, one accent, cool paper, warm cream reserved for evidence only. editorial, not corporate.</Direction>
</Directions>

what shipped is a small, strict system: a single ink (`#16294A`) and a single accent (`#4E73B0`) on cool paper, warm cream held back for stat and evidence sections only, Cormorant Garamond for display against Hanken Grotesk for everything functional, a 1240 grid on an 8-point scale, transform-only motion, and a quiet engraved-contour texture instead of 3d clip-art. one accent, one voice, nothing competing for attention. i built it as tokens in code, not a swatch page.

<CsSection num="05" label="Systems Design" title={<>designing <em>trust</em> into the product.</>}>

the hard part of asnd is not the screens, it is the trust mechanics underneath. the matching engine and backend are my co-founder Anurag's systems work. mine was the ux around them, so complex trust rules feel simple. the promise, in one comparison:

<Split
  aLabel="today"
  a={<>need a creator, search and dm dozens, chase replies, settle. <em>two to four weeks.</em></>}
  bLabel="with asnd"
  b={<>describe the brief, get a ranked shortlist that fits, connect. <em>two to four hours.</em></>}
/>

three decisions carried the most weight.

**the wedge is a multi-role brief.** post one opportunity with several roles, each its own sub-brief and shortlist. briefs fail closed if a role has no references, which raises quality by design. one brief, several roles cast in an afternoon.

**the research is encoded as ranking weights.** "fit, not followers" isn't a slogan, it is the rubric. i turned the interviews into the numbers the system ranks on:

<Rubric
  items={[
    { k: "niche / audience fit", v: 0.25 },
    { k: "engagement quality", v: 0.20 },
    { k: "brand / aesthetic vibe", v: 0.20 },
    { k: "location", v: 0.15 },
    { k: "availability", v: 0.10 },
    { k: "experience", v: 0.07 },
    { k: "follower count", v: 0.03, locked: true }
  ]}
/>

**trust, not ai, is the headline.** the matching model exists, but it is demoted to a quiet "ask" verb, never an "ai-powered" banner, because in a synthetic-influencer climate an ai headline reduces trust with the exact buyers we want. connection requests are rate-limited with no bulk send, and an agency that is both buyer and supplier never sees a competitor's confidential brief. the rules are enforced by the system, not by good behavior.

</CsSection>

<CsSection num="06" label="The Build" title={<>designed <em>and built</em>, in code.</>}>

i didn't stop at screens. asnd is a real product, and what i designed and built is its working prototype: the whole role-aware app in react and typescript, every flow running live over seed data while Anurag brings the backend online for beta. building in the real medium let me pressure-test flows end to end instead of faking them in a static file.

i built it with ai (Claude Code) as a design-engineering partner: describing intent, reviewing the built result in a live browser, refining, and committing incrementally, roughly 60 commits of traceable history. this is the thing i want to be known for, the overlap of research, design intuition, and ai collaboration, shipped as a running product rather than a mockup. the app carries three pillars:
